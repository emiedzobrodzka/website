01 — About

A researcher at the intersection of media, mind, and development

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Education at Utrecht University, where I work within the interdisciplinary Youth & Digitalisation (JEDi) consortium on the theme of digital media use and equal opportunities. My work asks how growing up with screens shapes young people — and how differences in access and use can widen or narrow inequalities in development.

I completed my PhD, “Traces of Violent Video Gameplay in Adolescent Development,” at the Department of Communication Science, Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam in 2023, supported by an NWO Research Talent Grant. For that project I individually tested more than 500 adolescent boys, combining behavioural tasks with electroencephalography (EEG) and event-related potentials (ERP) to measure emotion recognition, perspective-taking, inhibitory control, and empathy for pain. The central finding: the impact of violent gameplay on these skills is limited and temporary — a nuanced message that pushes back against alarmist narratives.

I care deeply about open, transparent science. I am an active member of the Open Science Community Utrecht and a core team member of Young NeurolabNL, and I work across the full knowledge chain — from fundamental research to interventions and tools that practitioners, parents, and educators can actually use.

02 — Research

Research themes

My research spans media psychology, developmental psychology, and social neuroscience. Across projects I ask a common question: what does digital media do to the developing mind — and for whom?

🎮

Violent video games & the adolescent brain

Using behavioural tasks and ERP/EEG, I examine whether and how violent gameplay relates to emotion recognition, empathy for pain, and inhibitory control in adolescents — separating short-term effects from lasting change.

📱

Social media, well-being & self-control

I study how platforms such as TikTok relate to well-being, sleep, academic performance, and body image — with attention to how we measure media use in the first place.

⚖️

Digital media & equal opportunities

Within the JEDi consortium, I investigate how inequalities in digital media access and use among children and adolescents translate into differences in social, cognitive, and identity development.

🧠

Neuroscience & education

I connect neuroscientific insight to learning and self-regulation, including game-based approaches to health literacy and vaccine confidence in youth.

Current projects

Youth & Digitalisation (JEDi)

2023 – 2029

A large interdisciplinary consortium (funded through the NWO / Dutch Research Agenda) examining mechanisms in children's and young people's digital media use and how these relate to development and inequality — combining fundamental, applied, and practice-oriented research into tested interventions and tools.
